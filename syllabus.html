Course description

This introductory course is for college students and anyone curious about the universe. It tells the story of modern astronomy and the cosmic perspective: seeing ourselves and our planet as one part of a much larger universe. We cover our planet, our star, our solar system, our galaxy, the view of the sky, other stars, other galaxies, the history of the universe, and the tools and methods astronomers use to study them.

Prerequisites: MATH 015 and READ 095, or passing scores in computation and reading on the Basic Skills test.

Course materials: This website replaces a printed textbook. All readings, simulations, and practice quizzes are free and available here.

Learning outcomes

After completing this general education physics course, you will be able to:

  1. Communicate information and ideas about the basic concepts of the universe clearly and effectively, in writing and in speech (communication and information literacy).
  2. Apply the scientific method, fundamental principles of physics, and mathematical techniques to solve problems (mathematical and scientific reasoning, critical thinking).

Homework

  • Homework is posted in Canvas for each module.
  • Homework is individual work and must be done alone.
  • Answer every question by hand.
  • Answers copied, directly or indirectly, from books, the internet, solution manuals, AI tools, or another student receive a zero.
  • Each of the 13 assignments is worth 3 points, 39 points in all; 34 of those points count toward your grade.

The self-check quizzes on this site are ungraded practice. Use them as often as you like.

Exams

  • The midterm is given in class after Module 6 and covers Modules 1–6. The final is given in class after Module 13 and covers Modules 7–13.
  • If you miss the midterm, you may take it the following week, with a maximum score of 70%. If it is not made up within one week of the original date, a failing grade is recorded.
  • There is no makeup for the final.
  • If you know at least two weeks in advance that you cannot take a scheduled exam for a justifiable reason, talk with your instructor about other arrangements.
  • Exam grades may be curved based on overall class performance.

Course outline

  1. Developing perspective: our place in the universe, the sky, the science of astronomy (Modules 1–2)
  2. Key concepts: timekeeping, motion, energy, gravity, light, telescopes (Modules 2–3)
  3. The solar system: planets, formation, geology, atmospheres, jovian systems, small bodies, exoplanets (Modules 4–6)
  4. Space, time, and gravity: relativity and quantum ideas (Modules 7–8)
  5. Stars: the Sun, stellar properties, the birth and death of stars (Modules 9–11)
  6. Galaxies and beyond: our galaxy, other galaxies, cosmology (Modules 11–13)
